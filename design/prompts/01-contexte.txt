You are the art director for a small Windows desktop app called Flashwatch. I will
ask you for a visual direction and then for a series of UI mockup images. Read this
brief first and keep it for the whole conversation.

WHAT THE APP IS
Flashwatch watches the player's screen during a League of Legends match. When the
game prints in the chat that an enemy has used a summoner spell, the app reads that
line with OCR and starts a countdown, then shows those countdowns in a small
always-on-top overlay drawn over the game. It never injects into the game, never
reads its memory, never sends input: it only reads pixels already on screen. It is
not affiliated with Riot Games in any way.

WHO USES IT
League players, mid-match, under stress, glancing for a fraction of a second. The
single most important pixel in the product is a countdown like "4:23" read at a
glance over a busy, bright, moving game.

THE SURFACES YOU WILL DESIGN
1. THE OVERLAY - drawn over the game, translucent, always on top, click-through.
   Three interchangeable displays of the same data, chosen by the user:
   a) CHRONO TRACK: a wide shallow horizontal strip, roughly 560x78 px, sitting at
      the top centre of the screen. A thin rail runs across it; each cooldown is a
      circular champion portrait with a small circular spell badge on its corner,
      hanging under the rail at the point representing how far through the cooldown
      it is - entering at the left when the spell is cast, arriving at the right as
      it comes back up. The countdown text sits under each portrait.
   b) FIXED CARDS: a compact strip, roughly 460x104 px. One card per cooldown, in a
      slot that never moves: a circular portrait with a PROGRESS RING closing around
      it clockwise from the top, a spell badge on its lower right, a tiny uppercase
      role label above (TOP, JUNGLE, MID, ADC, SUPPORT) and the countdown below.
   c) COMPACT ROWS: a tall narrow panel, roughly 300x330 px. One row per cooldown:
      portrait, spell badge, champion name, spell name, countdown right-aligned, and
      a thin horizontal progress gauge along the bottom of the row. Rows are grouped
      under small uppercase role headings.
2. THE CONTROL WINDOW - a normal desktop window, about 620x680 px. A header with
   the app mark, its name, a one-line tagline and a small state pill; a vertical
   navigation column on the left with four entries; a scrolling content area of
   cards; a footer with two buttons.
3. THE SETUP GUIDE - a 650x740 px window shown once on first run. Six steps, each
   with a large schematic illustration above a title, a paragraph and a note box.
   A footer with a six-dot stepper and back/next buttons.
4. THE MARK - a small icon that works at 16 px in the Windows notification area and
   at 34 px in a window header. Today it is a dark disc with a cyan rim and two
   white clock hands. Deliberately abstract: no fantasy iconography, nothing that
   could be mistaken for a game's branding.

THREE STATES THAT CARRY MEANING AND MUST STAY DISTINGUISHABLE AT A GLANCE
- counting down (neutral, the default)
- nearly back up, under 30 seconds (a warning colour)
- back up / READY (a positive colour)
Two extra marks: a small "?" chip on a spell badge when the app only inferred which
spell was used, and a "~" before a countdown that is an estimate.

HARD TECHNICAL CONSTRAINTS - these decide what you may propose
- Everything is drawn with Qt/PySide6: QPainter for the overlay and the
  illustrations, Qt Style Sheets for the desktop window widgets.
- AVAILABLE: flat fills, linear/radial/conical gradients, per-pixel alpha, rounded
  rectangles, circles, arcs and rings, clipping to arbitrary paths, hairlines,
  layered translucency, painted (hand-drawn) shadows and glows, bundled TTF fonts.
- NOT AVAILABLE: background blur behind translucent panels (there is no
  backdrop-filter, so no frosted glass over the game), CSS box-shadow or
  text-shadow on widgets, per-frame image filters, shaders, animated video
  textures, photographic imagery or 3D renders inside the UI.
- The overlay repaints about ten times a second over a running game and must stay
  under 3% CPU. Nothing expensive per frame.
- Any typeface you recommend must be free for embedding (SIL OFL or Apache-2.0) and
  must be named precisely so it can be bundled.
- No Riot Games artwork, no champion art, no logos, no in-game screenshots. In the
  mockups, champion portraits are plain grey circles with two initials.

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
Precision over prose. When you propose a direction, give me exact values: hex
colours with a role for each, typeface names with weights and sizes, corner radii,
stroke widths, opacity values, spacing steps. When you generate an image, it is a
reference for shape, palette, texture and hierarchy - I will rebuild it in code, so
it must be buildable with the tools listed above.

Acknowledge this brief in three lines, then wait for my next message.
