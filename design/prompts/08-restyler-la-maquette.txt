Here is a self-contained HTML mockup of every surface of the desktop app described
in my previous message. Read it, then restyle it.

WHAT TO CHANGE
Only the tokens in the first :root block - the ones prefixed --fw-. Their names map
one-to-one onto constants in the app's source, so the names must not change: no
renaming, no removing, no adding. Keep every one of them, change their values.

You may also change the small number of CSS rules that shape a component's
structure (a border radius here, a gradient direction there, the weight of a rule)
- but every rule you touch must stay reproducible with: flat fills, linear/radial/
conical gradients, per-pixel alpha, rounded rectangles, circles, arcs, clipping to
paths, hairlines, and a hand-painted offset shadow. Nothing else exists in Qt.

WHAT NOT TO CHANGE
- The second :root block, prefixed --doc-. That is the page's own chrome and it
  must stay readable whatever you do to the product.
- The HTML structure, the class names, and the text content.
- The three state colours must remain instantly distinguishable from each other at
  a glance: counting, soon (under 30 seconds), ready. They carry meaning, not
  decoration.

WHAT TO ADD, NOWHERE
No backdrop-filter, no box-shadow on a widget, no transition, no transform, no
web font URL, no external asset, no photography, no JavaScript.

THE TEST THAT MATTERS
The overlay sits over a live game. Section 03 of the page has a background switcher:
medium, dark and bright. Your palette must keep the countdown readable on ALL
THREE. The current one fails on bright - fix that as part of the direction rather
than ignoring it.

DELIVERABLE
The complete modified HTML file, in one code block, ready to save over the original.
Then, underneath it, a short list of what you changed and why - one line per
decision - and explicitly name anything you had to compromise on because of the Qt
constraints.
